AML/CTF Policy and Procedures

Last updated: November 16, 2026

1. Purpose and Scope

This document describes the position of UTIL ATIVIDADES WEB LTDA, CNPJ 47.509.953/0001-43, operator of verumcrypto.com ("Verum"), in relation to the prevention of money laundering, the financing of terrorism, and the financing of the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction (AML/CTF/CPF), and sets out who performs which control in the context of the Verum Wallet application.

This policy is deliberately written to describe only what actually exists. It does not claim controls that Verum does not operate. Where a control is performed by a third party, this document says so expressly and identifies that third party.

2. Nature of the Service

Verum Wallet is self-custody interface software. Verum:

  • does not hold customer accounts and does not maintain balances on behalf of users;
  • does not have access to private keys, seed phrases, or user funds;
  • does not receive, hold, custody, or transmit fiat currency at any moment;
  • does not offer any fiat on-ramp or off-ramp service (purchase or sale of digital assets against fiat currency);
  • does not act as an exchange, brokerage, payment institution, money transmitter, or financial intermediary.

The QR payment functionality ("Global Pay") is non-custodial software only: it facilitates direct wallet-to-wallet payments on the blockchain, and funds never pass through Verum.

Because Verum never holds user assets or fiat funds, it has no technical ability to block, freeze, seize, return, or reverse any transaction or amount. Any statement to the contrary would be false.

3. Swap Providers and Their Controls

Verum Wallet does not offer fiat on-ramp or off-ramp services. The token exchange (swap) functionalities made available through Verum Wallet are executed by the following independent third parties:

  • Jupiter (jup.ag) and Raydium (raydium.io) — decentralized, on-chain protocols on the Solana blockchain. The user interacts directly with their smart contracts; the operations involve only digital assets already held in the user's self-custody wallet, with no fiat leg, and all movements are publicly recorded and auditable on the blockchain;
  • ChangeNOW (changenow.io) — an independent exchange service used for cross-chain swaps. ChangeNOW operates its own AML/CTF programme, under which it may, according to its own risk rules, require identity verification (KYC) for specific transactions, screen against sanctions lists, hold or reject suspicious operations, and report suspicious activity to the competent authorities under the law applicable to it.

Where such processes apply, they take place entirely within the provider's environment. Verum does not collect, receive, review, store, or have access to identity documents, verification data, or the files underlying these decisions. Verum has no influence over, and no power to review or overturn, any decision made by these providers.

4. What Verum Actually Does

The measures below are the full extent of what Verum maintains. They are governance and access measures, not financial-institution controls:

  • Provider selection: before and during an integration, we check that any centralised third-party swap provider we make available (currently ChangeNOW) maintains a published AML/KYC programme.
  • Structural non-custody: our architecture is designed so that neither fiat funds nor user keys pass through Verum. This is our main risk mitigation measure.
  • Acceptable use policy: the Terms of Service expressly prohibit the use of the application for money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud, sanctions evasion, and the splitting of operations to avoid verification thresholds.
  • Restricted jurisdictions: we publish and keep updated the list of restricted jurisdictions in Section 6, and we do not promote the service in those territories.
  • Interface access restriction: where we become aware — through a report, a communication from a provider, or a lawful order — that an address or user is involved in unlawful activity or is subject to sanctions, we may restrict access to our interface and discontinue support. This measure affects only our software; it does not affect assets on the blockchain, which remain under the user's exclusive control.
  • Response to authorities: we respond to valid, properly founded requests from competent authorities, within the limits of the law, providing only the limited data we actually hold (described in the Privacy Policy).
  • Internal guidance: our support team is instructed never to request, receive, or store identity documents, seed phrases, or banking data, and to redirect all verification matters to the respective provider's official channels (e.g. ChangeNOW).
  • Periodic review: this policy and the provider integrations are reviewed at least annually, or whenever there is a material change in the regulatory framework or in a provider.

5. What Verum Does Not Do

For the avoidance of doubt and to prevent any misleading expectation, Verum does not:

  • perform KYC, identity verification, or the collection of identification documents;
  • carry out customer due diligence (CDD/EDD) or source-of-funds and source-of-wealth assessments;
  • screen users against sanctions or PEP lists, nor maintain its own screening tooling;
  • operate blockchain transaction monitoring, wallet-address risk scoring, or on-chain analytics;
  • submit suspicious transaction reports or cash-transaction reports to any financial intelligence unit in relation to swaps executed by third-party providers — where such a duty exists, it lies with the provider that executes them (e.g. ChangeNOW);
  • maintain a certified compliance department, an internal AML auditor, or an independent testing programme of the kind required of financial institutions;
  • freeze, block, confiscate, or return funds — which is technically impossible in a self-custody model;
  • hold a payment-institution licence, an exchange registration, or an authorisation as a virtual asset service provider.

Verum does not act as an exchange or payment institution and therefore does not undertake the customer due diligence duties assigned to those entities. Should the applicable law of any jurisdiction in which we operate come to attribute such duties to interface software providers, we will adjust this policy and our operation before continuing to offer the service in that jurisdiction.

6. Prohibited Activities and Restricted Jurisdictions

It is expressly prohibited to use Verum Wallet to conceal or disguise the nature, origin, location, disposition, movement, or ownership of assets derived from unlawful activity; to finance terrorism or the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction; to evade sanctions; to carry out fraud, extortion, or ransomware payments; to trade in illicit goods or services; or to act for a third party without disclosure.

The service is not offered to persons resident in, located in, or acting on behalf of persons in: Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, and the Crimea, Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson and Zaporizhzhia regions of Ukraine; any territory subject to comprehensive sanctions imposed by the UN, EU, UK (OFSI), or US (OFAC); and any jurisdiction listed by FATF/GAFI as a "High-Risk Jurisdiction Subject to a Call for Action".

ChangeNOW applies its own, broader list of country and transaction restrictions, which may change without prior notice. The list of the provider executing the operation is the one that determines whether a swap can be completed.

7. Reports and Contact

If you identify any use of Verum Wallet for unlawful purposes, or if you are an authority seeking information, contact us at contato@verumcrypto.com. Reports are reviewed within the limits of the data we hold.

Matters relating to identity verification, documents, or blocked or held swaps must be addressed directly to the provider executing the operation — in the case of ChangeNOW, through the channels published at changenow.io. Verum has no access to those processes and cannot intervene in them.

8. Updates

This policy may be updated to reflect changes in the regulatory framework, in the swap providers, or in our operation. The version in force is the one published on this page, with the date indicated at the top.
